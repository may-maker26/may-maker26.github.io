About Me

Pursuing excellence in plasma physics research

Academic Background

I am a physics major at Miami Dade College with a deep fascination for the complex behavior of plasma systems and nonlinear dynamics. My academic journey has been shaped by a genuine curiosity about the underlying order hidden within seemingly chaotic phenomena, and a desire to develop the mathematical and computational tools to reveal it.

Through my undergraduate research with Professor Adolfo Mendez, I have built hands-on expertise in computational physics, nonlinear time-series analysis, and statistical validation. My work centers on applying phase-space reconstruction and correlation dimension techniques to solar radio burst data, asking whether the irregularity we observe in these emissions is truly random, or whether something more structured lies beneath.

Research Interests

⚡

Plasma Physics

Investigating nonlinear dynamics and deterministic chaos in astrophysical and laboratory plasmas, with a focus on solar radio emissions.

◎

Turbulence & MHD Instabilities

Exploring magnetohydrodynamic phenomena and their role in plasma confinement, stability, and energy transport.

🎓

Predictive Modeling

Developing computational frameworks for forecasting complex plasma behavior and space weather events using data-driven approaches.

Long-term Goals

My goal is to pursue a PhD in plasma physics, where I can keep exploring the intersection of nonlinear dynamics, computational modeling, and experimental research. I am particularly drawn to turbulence in magnetically confined plasmas and its implications for fusion energy, a problem that sits at the edge of our current understanding and has real consequences for the future of clean energy.

I want to build a research career that connects theory, computation, and experiment: from astrophysical phenomena like solar radio bursts to practical challenges in fusion reactor design. More than anything, I want to keep asking hard questions and developing the rigorous methods to answer them, and to do that alongside a community of scientists.
