About This Manuscript

This manuscript represents a synthesis of my undergraduate research on nonlinear dynamics and deterministic chaos in solar radio burst spikes. Co-authored with Professor Adolfo Mendez, it presents our methodological approach, analytical framework, and findings applying nonlinear time-series analysis to astrophysical plasma phenomena.

The work emphasizes computational physics methods for uncovering hidden structure in complex systems and contributes to understanding solar radio emissions and space weather dynamics.

Abstract

Solar radio burst spikes represent one of the most enigmatic phenomena in solar physics, characterized by rapid temporal fluctuations on millisecond timescales. This study employs nonlinear time-series analysis techniques to test for deterministic chaos in these emissions.

Using phase-space reconstruction via Takens embedding and correlation dimension analysis, we find statistically significant evidence consistent with high-dimensional chaotic attractors underlying the observed spike patterns. Statistical testing indicates these signatures cannot be explained by linear stochastic processes alone.

These results suggest solar radio burst spikes may arise from nonlinear plasma instabilities exhibiting deterministic chaotic behavior, with implications for understanding the microphysics of solar radio emissions and for improving space weather forecasting frameworks. The methodology is transferable to other complex astrophysical and laboratory plasma systems.

Key Contributions

◎

Methodology

Applied correlation dimension analysis within a nonlinear time-series pipeline for spike burst data.

✓

Validation

Used nonlinear parameters such as Kolmogorov Entropy, Hurst Exponent, and Deterministic factor to verify chaos structure is intrinsic rather than an observational artifact.

⚡

Physical Insight

Interpreted results in the context of plasma instabilities and wave–particle interactions.
